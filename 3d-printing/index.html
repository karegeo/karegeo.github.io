Notes from the bench

  • Thin, wide parts warp before they fail. They lift at the corners long before anything else goes wrong; a brim and a slower first layer fix most of it.
  • Surface finish can be the experiment. When a part has to interact with a fluid or a surface, the top-layer finish and any stringing change the behaviour you are trying to measure, not just how it looks. Print those faces down on a smooth plate.
  • Print a batch in one go. Parts from different sessions differ enough in mass and edge quality to show up in results — if a set has to be identical, it has to come off the plate together.
  • Measure, don't trust the slicer. Nominal and printed dimensions drift apart by more than you would like, especially on small parts; calibrate against a printed test piece.
  • Keep the model parametric if you can. Every experiment eventually needs the same part at a different size, and a script regenerates it where a mesh has to be redrawn.

Software

Models are authored parametrically where the geometry allows it — mostly in OpenSCAD, so a part can be regenerated at a different size instead of being redrawn — and exported to STL for slicing. Sources live alongside the projects they belong to on GitHub.
